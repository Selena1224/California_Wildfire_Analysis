# California Wildfire Analysis: Trends, Damage Patterns, and Structural Risk Factors

This project presents a data-driven analysis of California wildfires from 2013 to 2024. It explores how wildfire activity has evolved over time, the increasing property exposure to risk, and how specific building features influence a structure’s likelihood of surviving fire events. The goal is to provide meaningful insights for urban planners, policymakers, and emergency management professionals.

## Project Objective

The objective of this project is to identify temporal and geographic wildfire trends, evaluate risk patterns related to property value and building characteristics, and offer practical recommendations for improving fire resilience across California communities.

## Tools and Technologies

- Tableau Public – for data visualization and spatial analysis
- Excel – for data preprocessing and transformation
- Animation (MP4) – for temporal mapping of wildfire expansion
- Data source: California Post-Fire Parcel Inspection Dataset (2013–2024)

## Key Business Questions

1. How have wildfire incidents changed geographically and over time?
2. How has the frequency of wildfires and the total value of exposed properties evolved between 2013 and 2024?
3. What is the seasonal distribution of wildfire events, and how has the fire season shifted?
4. What is the relationship between property value and wildfire damage severity?
5. How do structural features such as roof type, siding, eaves, and vent screens impact damage outcomes?

## Summary of Findings

- Wildfire activity has significantly expanded since 2017, affecting both rural and suburban communities.
- Property exposure to wildfire risk has increased, with high-value homes showing greater resilience due to construction quality and defensible space.
- The fire season has lengthened, with a noticeable rise in incidents occurring between June and November.
- Structural features such as enclosed eaves, metal or tile roofs, and fine mesh vent screens correlate with significantly lower destruction rates.
- Mid- and lower-value homes experienced higher destruction rates, highlighting disparities in access to fire mitigation resources.

## Recommendations

- Expand and enforce fire-resistant construction standards in wildfire-prone areas
- Implement grant programs and public funding to support retrofitting in vulnerable neighborhoods
- Develop and promote public education on home-hardening strategies and ember-resistant construction
- Align fire prevention policy with ethical principles of fairness, stewardship, and community resilience
